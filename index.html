Real work

The report gave a number. The tickets gave a name.

One month at a commercial bank’s IT service desk, read from its own ClickUp tickets. The bank is not named. The lesson is not bank-specific.

BeforeWhat the monthly report showed

  • 414 complaints raised
  • 41 past the turnaround target
  • One total for the whole region, and no way to tell who was holding what

AfterWhat the ticket register showed

  • 97 still open
  • 26 of them with no owner at all
  • Every ticket carried an age, a branch and an owner, so the oldest unowned work had a name

The fix was not a better chart. It was putting a name against a queue nobody owned.

Client unnamed at the bank’s request. Figures verified against its ticket export. No results beyond the analysis are claimed.

Analysis types

Questions I answer with data

Not only service desks. The method is the same on any operational dataset.

  • Service-level and backlog analysis

    Where targets break, by team, branch and priority, with unowned work separated from work in hand.

  • Aging and escalation analysis

    Age bands, repeat categories and escalation flags that show where capacity is quietly consumed.

  • Variance and driver analysis

    Actual versus plan decomposed by region, product or owner, so a gap can be attributed and acted on.

  • Trend and flow analysis

    Opened against closed over time, net backlog movement and seasonality instead of one static snapshot.
