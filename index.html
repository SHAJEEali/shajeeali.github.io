Real work

One month of tickets, one ownership gap.

A commercial bank’s Islamabad-region IT service desk, one month of ClickUp tickets. The bank is not named. Every figure below comes from its own export.

414complaints raised in one month
97still active, each with an owner, an age and a TAT status
26active password resets with no assignee, 19 already past TAT

What the analysis changed

The queue was already being reported. Aggregate views showed 41 TAT breaches across 414 complaints, and management could see the total without seeing who owned it. Rebuilding the register from the raw tickets put an owner, an age and a TAT status on each of the 97 active ones.

That split one number into a decision: password resets alone held 26 active tickets with no assignee, 19 of them already escalated. The fix was not a better chart. It was a named owner for a queue nobody had.

Client unnamed at the bank’s request. Figures verified against the ticket export. No results beyond the analysis are claimed.

Analysis types

Questions I answer with data

Not only service desks. The method is the same on any operational dataset.

  • Service-level and backlog analysis

    Where targets break, by team, branch and priority, with unowned work separated from work in hand.

  • Aging and escalation analysis

    Age bands, repeat categories and escalation flags that show where capacity is quietly consumed.

  • Variance and driver analysis

    Actual versus plan decomposed by region, product or owner, so a gap can be attributed and acted on.

  • Trend and flow analysis

    Opened against closed over time, net backlog movement and seasonality instead of one static snapshot.
